01 / Meet M87*

M87* is a supermassive black hole surrounded by hot, magnetized matter. Its powerful relativistic jet makes it one of the most important objects for studying black-hole astrophysics.

ANIMATED SCIENTIFIC VISUALIZATION · NOT TO SCALE

What is it?

A supermassive black hole with a mass of approximately 6.5 billion Suns.

Where is it?

At the centre of the giant elliptical galaxy Messier 87.

Why is it famous?

It was the first black hole to be imaged by the Event Horizon Telescope in 2019.

What are you looking at?

Dark centre: The black-hole shadow, not a solid surface.

Bright ring: Emission from hot plasma shaped by gravity and light bending.

Accretion disk: Hot matter orbiting the black hole.

Relativistic jet: A narrow outflow of energetic plasma moving close to the speed of light.

02 / Anatomy of M87*

Explore the main regions around the black hole and understand how matter and radiation behave in its environment.

1. Singularity

The central region where classical General Relativity predicts a singularity. A quantum-gravity description remains unknown.

2. Event Horizon

The boundary beyond which no future-directed signal can escape to distant observers.

3. Photon Region

In the non-rotating approximation, unstable circular photon orbits occur at 1.5 Schwarzschild radii.

4. Accretion Flow

Hot, magnetized plasma moving around the black hole. It emits radiation and can help power outflows.

5. Black-Hole Shadow

A dark image feature produced by light capture and gravitational lensing.

6. Relativistic Jet

A highly collimated outflow of plasma associated with magnetic fields and the rotating black-hole environment.

The jet is not emitted from inside the event horizon. It is produced in the surrounding magnetized environment.

03 / Rotating Black Hole — Kerr Model

A rotating black hole changes the geometry of spacetime. The Kerr solution describes an idealized rotating, uncharged black hole.

Spin parameter: 0.70

Rotating Kerr black-hole model.
Dimensionless spin a* = cJ / GM²

J = angular momentum

M = black-hole mass

c = speed of light

G = gravitational constant

What is frame dragging?

A rotating black hole changes the spacetime around it. This produces an effect called frame dragging, in which nearby inertial frames are dragged around with the rotation.

What is the ergosphere?

Outside the event horizon of a rotating black hole is a region where an observer cannot remain stationary relative to distant stars. This region is called the ergosphere.

Why use the Kerr solution?

M87* is an astrophysical black-hole candidate associated with a powerful jet. A rotating model is useful for exploring possible jet-power mechanisms, although this simulation does not measure its actual spin.

04 / Relativistic Jet Formation

M87* launches a powerful jet of energetic plasma. Magnetic fields in the rotating black-hole environment are believed to play a major role in launching and collimating these outflows.

Speed: 90% of c

How is the jet formed?

1. Gas and plasma accumulate in the accretion flow.

2. Magnetic fields become important in the inner region.

3. Electromagnetic processes help launch energetic plasma.

4. The outflow becomes collimated into a narrow jet.

5. Particles travel outward at relativistic speeds.

Why is it important?

Relativistic jets transfer energy and momentum into their surroundings. They can influence gas in and around galaxies.

05 / General Relativity & Spacetime

Einstein's General Relativity describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime caused by matter and energy.

Einstein Field Equation Gμν = 8πG Tμν / c⁴

Spacetime geometry is related to the distribution of matter and energy.

Space

Mass changes the geometry of spatial distances.

Time

Gravitational fields affect the rate at which clocks run.

Motion

Objects follow geodesics through curved spacetime.

Why does M87* help?

Its shadow, surrounding plasma and relativistic jet provide observations with which scientists investigate strong gravity and black-hole environments.

06 / Gravitational Lensing & Light Paths

Light follows null geodesics in spacetime. Near M87*, gravitational curvature changes its trajectory.

Distance: 5 Rs

What happens to light?

1. Light approaches the black hole.

2. Curved spacetime changes its path.

3. It can escape, be strongly deflected or be captured.

4. Light crossing the event horizon cannot return.

09 / Black-Hole Thermodynamics

Black holes are connected to temperature, entropy and thermodynamic laws. Their thermodynamics combines gravity, quantum theory and statistical physics.

1. Accretion heating

Gas and plasma can heat up as they spiral inward and release gravitational energy.

2. Hawking temperature

Quantum field theory predicts that black holes emit thermal Hawking radiation.

3. Entropy

Black-hole entropy is proportional to the area of the event horizon.

4. Energy transfer

Accretion and jets transfer energy into the surrounding environment.

Hawking temperature T = ℏc³ / 8πGMkB

For an ideal non-rotating black hole.

Bekenstein-Hawking entropy S = kB c³ A / 4Gℏ

A = event-horizon area.

M87* has an extremely low predicted Hawking temperature. Its observed astrophysical emission is dominated by its hot surrounding plasma, not detectable Hawking radiation.

Why study thermodynamics?

It connects General Relativity with quantum physics and helps scientists investigate the fundamental nature of gravity, entropy and information.
